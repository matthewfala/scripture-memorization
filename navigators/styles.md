## Rationale

- A: Fixed point given by Procedure 00 — memorization has begun; never altered.
- B: Gospel's home vocal texture is call-and-response, and uptempo contemporary gospel at 112 BPM is the genre's natural register; maximal contrast with A's quiet 1970s folk, and gospel is reliably fully sung with strong melodic hooks.
- C: The 1980s is soft rock's home era and a slow solo-lead ballad at 68 BPM is the genre's natural form; solo lead keeps the vocal texture distinct from A's harmonies and B's call-and-response, and the ballad form guarantees a melody-forward line.
- D: Bluegrass lives at driving tempos (126 BPM) and the 1990s roots-revival is a natural production era; a male-female duet is idiomatic to the genre while occupying the last mid-texture vocal band, giving the sharpest tempo contrast in the set.
- E: A hymn sung by full choir over pipe organ is the genre's definitional texture; a stately 96 BPM processional tempo and reverberant 1960s cathedral sound are its home register, and choral hymnody is inherently fully sung, filling the remaining bands on every axis.
- S1: G10 is adjacent to A's G1, so per the procedure it goes to the grouping most distant from A on the other axes — S1 sits at maximal tempo distance (T5 vs T2) and maximal era distance (E5 vs E2) and shares no band with A. A driving reel tempo (128 BPM), contemporary production, and full choir (Celtic choral tradition) are all natural to the genre, and the majestic register suits the "Getting To Know God" subject.
- S2: Early-1960s production and close group harmony are doo-wop's definitional home; a slow 12/8 ballad at 66 BPM is its classic love-song form, fitting the "Growing In Love" subject, and doo-wop harmony is inherently fully sung and melody-forward.
- S3: A gentle 78 BPM sway is bossa nova's natural pulse and a soft male-female duet is idiomatic (the classic Rio duet records); the polished mid-1980s Brazilian studio era keeps it clear of the genre's 1960s origin, which S2 needed, while the calm intimate register suits "Growing in Faith".
- S4: Medium-up swing at 116 BPM fronted by a solo crooner is the genre's natural shape, and the 1990s swing-revival is a strong, recognizable production era; the exuberant big-band register suits "Growing in Victory". (E4 with V4 was rejected — it would have shared two bands with D.)
- S5: Roots reggae's home is 1970s Kingston analog production, an upbeat 98 BPM skank is authentic to the genre's brighter side, and a lead answered by backing singers is its natural texture; call-and-response enacts proclamation, suiting "Sharing Your Faith".
